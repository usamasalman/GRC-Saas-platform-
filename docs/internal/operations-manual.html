01

How permission is decided

Every request passes four independent gates. They are not alternatives — all four apply, in order, and any one can stop the request. Understanding this stack explains almost every "why can't I do that?" question.

Gate 1
Are you who you say you are?

A valid token is required. Users who must change their password are stopped here with 428. Impersonated sessions are allowed to read but every write is refused.

Gate 2
Does your role grant this privilege?

Each route names the capability it needs. Your role grants a set of capability keys. No key, no entry — and the error names the missing privilege so an administrator knows exactly what to grant.

CAPABILITY_DENIED: Your role "Risk Manager" does not grant "plan-and-execute-an-audit".
Gate 3
Is this record inside your world?

Your tenant's operating model resolves to a set of tenant IDs. Every query is filtered to that set before it runs, so a branch user cannot reach a sibling branch's data even with the right privilege. Legitimate group-level cross-tenant reads are logged as such.

Gate 4
Would this create a conflict of interest?

Separation of duties is checked last, against the audit history of the record itself. This is why holding the right privilege is not enough — the system knows you raised this finding, so it will not let you close it.

SOD_VIOLATION: Independent closure required: whoever raised an issue cannot close it.
The distinction that matters

Privilege is about job function — can this kind of person do this kind of thing? Separation of duties is about this specific record — has this person already played a conflicting part in this case? A GRC Manager holds both the risk and audit privileges, yet is still blocked from validating a corrective action they personally owned.

02

A ticket, end to end

Your worked example. Every one of the 42 roles holds create-an-itsm-ticket, so anyone can start this. What happens afterwards depends on what they picked.

Requesterany role
Raises the ticket

Opens ITSM Service Desk, describes the problem, and chooses impact and urgency — or picks a catalogue item, which supplies those defaults.

Systemon save
Works out how urgent this really is

Priority is calculated on the server from the ITIL 3×3 matrix. A requester cannot declare their own ticket P1 — High impact plus High urgency produces P1, Medium and Medium produces P3, and so on.

priority = MATRIX[impact][urgency] · never taken from the request
Systemon save
Starts the clock

Two deadlines are set from the priority: when someone must respond, and when it must be resolved. A tenant's own SLA policy wins; otherwise the platform default applies.

P1 respond 15m / resolve 1h · P2 1h / 8h · P3 4h / 3d · P4 8h / 5d
Systemrouting
Sends it to a team — or to approval first

The catalogue item decides. Most items set an assignment group and the ticket opens as New. Four items carry an approval workflow instead: the ticket opens as Pending Approval and does not reach the queue until approvers have signed.

workflow-routed: production access · new starter · production change · security event
Approversif workflow
Approve in sequence

Each step names the privilege needed to act on it, so the right people see it in their workflow inbox. A production access request needs a manager review from someone holding add-a-user-with-role-based-access, then security approval from someone holding monitor-security-and-handle-incidents.

GET /api/itsm/workflows/inbox — an approver's queue
Service deskresolver role
Picks it up

Staff working Ticket Queues see it grouped by team, priority and SLA state. Assignment is a deliberate act — nothing auto-assigns to an individual, so a ticket sits with the team until a person takes it.

Systemfirst touch
Stops the response clock

The first assignment, status change, or comment from anyone other than the requester marks the ticket as responded to. Nothing else needs to be recorded.

Service deskwhile working
Writes two kinds of note

A comment is visible to the requester. A work note is internal and stored in a separate table entirely. The requester can never write an internal note on their own ticket — the system silently downgrades the attempt to a public comment.

Systemevery 5 min
Escalates anything that has run out of time

A background scan finds open tickets past their resolve deadline, marks them breached, raises the escalation level by one, and writes a permanent audit record. Breached tickets surface in SLA & Escalations.

TICKET_SLA_BREACHED · escalationLevel + 1
Service deskresolver role
Resolves it

Status moves to Resolved, Closed or Cancelled and the resolution timestamp is stamped. Good practice is to add a public comment first, because that comment is the only thing the requester will actually read.

03

How users find out

The platform tells people when something needs them. Notifications are written inside the same transaction as the action that caused them, so the platform can never announce a change that was rolled back.

What gets a notification

EventWho is toldBecause
Ticket assignedThe new assigneeThey now own it, with the resolve deadline in the message
Ticket resolved or closedThe requesterThe answer to "is my problem fixed?"
Public comment addedThe other partyRequester hears from the desk; the desk hears back
SLA breachedThe assigneeThe escalation is now on their record
Management responds to a findingWhoever raised itThe auditor learns whether it was accepted or disputed
Corrective action assignedThe action ownerThey now own remediation, with the due date
Issue closedThe raiser and the action ownerIndependent validation has completed

Two rules the notifier never breaks

  • Nobody is told about their own action. A service desk agent who assigns a ticket to themselves gets nothing — they already know.
  • Internal work notes never travel. Only public comments generate a notification, so a private note about a customer cannot reach that customer by accident.

Reading your inbox

GET  /api/notifications              your inbox, unread first
GET  /api/notifications/unread-count badge count
POST /api/notifications/:id/read     mark one read
POST /api/notifications/read-all     clear the badge

An inbox belongs to a person, not a tenant. It is the one place the tenant scope resolver is deliberately not used — a group-level role that can read an entire subtree still cannot read anyone else's notifications.

Still pull, not push

Delivery is in-app. There is no email or SMS integration, so a user who never opens the platform will not hear anything. Adding email later is a matter of reading the same notification rows — the events are already captured at the right moments.

The other workflows

The same actor-by-actor shape as the ticket trace, for the four other journeys that matter.

A policy from draft to acknowledged

Compliance Managerdocuments
Drafts and submits

Writes the policy, versions it, and sends it to the approval queue.

Compliance Approversigning
Reviews and approves

Picks it up from To Do & Approvals. The author cannot approve their own document.

SoD · dms-author-approver
Staff Employeepolicy
Acknowledges

Sees it under My Acknowledgements and confirms having read it. This record is the evidence a certification auditor asks for most often.

A control from library to working

GRC Managerstandards
Enables the standard

Turns on ISO 27001 for the tenant, making its clauses available for mapping.

GRC Managerimplementations
Creates the implementation

Assigns an owner and a review date. One control can satisfy clauses in several standards at once.

Control Ownerimplementations
Operates it and attaches evidence

Uploads what proves the control ran.

Someone elseimplementations
Validates it

Marks it Verified with an effectiveness rating. The owner cannot validate their own control.

SoD · grc-implementer-validator
Systemautomatic
Lowers residual risk

Every linked risk recalculates. Verified and Effective reduces likelihood by 2, PartiallyEffective by 1.

A risk being accepted

Risk Managerrisk
Proposes acceptance

Acceptance must be time-bound and reasoned — an open-ended acceptance is refused.

Systemappetite gate
Checks it against board appetite

If residual exceeds the approved tolerance for that category, acceptance is refused outright — the risk has to be treated down instead.

BEYOND_RISK_TOLERANCE
A second personrisk
Approves it

The risk owner cannot approve acceptance of their own risk.

A finding from raised to closed

Internal Auditoraudit
Raises the finding

Criterion, condition, cause and recommendation — all four required.

Managementrisk or implementations
Responds formally

Agree, Partially Agree or Disagree, with their own action plan. The auditor who raised it cannot write this response, and no corrective action can be assigned until it exists.

NO_MANAGEMENT_RESPONSE · SOD_VIOLATION
CAP ownerimplementations
Remediates and submits evidence

An evidence note is mandatory — it states what was fixed and where the proof sits.

A third personaudit
Validates and closes

Neither the raiser nor the remediator. If nobody qualifies, that is the system telling you the team is too small for independent assurance on this item.

08

Gaps to know about

One gap remains between what the role model implies and what the platform enforces. Two others described in earlier versions of this manual have since been closed.

Open · two thirds of privileges are inert

Twenty-one of the 34 privileges do not gate anything yet because their modules are unbuilt. A role showing eight privileges may only have three that currently do something. When demonstrating the role matrix, be clear that it describes the target design, not today's enforcement.

This resolves itself as each module ships — the capability keys are already reserved and assigned to the right roles, so nothing needs redesigning.

Closed

WasNow
Anyone could change any ticket. The update route accepted the create privilege, which all 42 roles hold, with no check that you were the requester or assignee — so any user could resolve or reassign anyone's ticket. Managing a ticket requires the resolve privilege. A requester may cancel their own ticket or comment on it; anything else returns REQUESTER_LIMITED. Touching a ticket you did not raise, without the resolve privilege, returns NOT_YOUR_TICKET.
Nothing was pushed to anyone. No notifications existed, so approvers never knew a request was waiting and requesters never knew their ticket was resolved. In-app notifications on seven events — see section 3. Written in the same transaction as the action, never sent to the person who performed it, and never carrying internal work notes.
